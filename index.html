Physics of Light Rays

When light, such as the laser simulated above, collides with a material, the path the ray travels depends on what the material is. For example, if light travelling in air collides with a mirror, the light wil bounce off the mirror. This is because mirrors are reflective.

When it comes to interacting with light, there are several possible classifications of materials.

Opaque materials are materials that let no light through. Most common objects are opaque, such as apples, rocks, chairs, tables, and many more. In general, if an object blocks light and you cannot see through it, it is most likely opaque.

Transparent materials are materials that allow light through. When light passes through a transparent material, the path of the light is bent. This phenomenon is called refraction.

Reflective materials are materials that reflect light. For example, a mirror is a reflective material. Many metals, such as aluminum, are also reflective.

Reflective materials are often also opaque, as they reflect light instead of letting the beam of light pass through. For example, a sheet of aluminum foil does not allow light through, so it would be classified as opaque. One exception is glass, which both reflects small amounts of light and allows most light through.


The angle at which light is refracted can be determined by Snell's law. This law, discovered in 1621 (about 400 years ago) describes how light is bent while entering and exiting materials.
(If you want to research more about Snell's law, you can click the link above.).

Snell's law implies that the amount light rays bend when entering a substance is determined by the refractive index of the substance. The refractive index of air is 1, and the refractive index of water is 1.3. Light bends less when traveling between materials withsimilar refractive indexes, and bends more if the refractive indexes are further apart.


One very useful way light refraction is used is in glasses. Since the lenses in glasses can bend light, by using curved lenses, light refraction can be used to bend light and focus images on the eyes. In prescription glasses, this is useful to counteract nearsightedness or farsightedness. In reading glasses, this is used to help focus on nearby objects (such as books) and prevent eye strain.
